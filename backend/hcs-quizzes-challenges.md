# Unit 1: Characteristics of Complex Systems

**#complexcausality**: Identify ways that multiple causes interact to produce complex effects. (C) CX

**#emergentproperties**: Identify emergent properties of complex systems and discern the interactions that give rise to them. (C) CX

**#levelsofanalysis**: Analyze characteristics or behavior of a complex system, integrating explanations found at different levels of analysis and their interactions. (H) CX

**#networks**: Apply network analysis to explain outcomes that arise out the structure of connections.(C) CX

**#systemdynamics**: Use the tools of phase spaces--including attractors, critical points, and basins--to describe and predict the ways a complex system changes over time. (C) CX

**#systemmapping**: Develop and analyze representations of complex systems by deconstructing them and conceptualizing their constituent parts in different ways, guided by an explanatory challenge. (C) CX

## 🪤 #fallacies

(Identify and correct logical fallacies.)

**Q9. Formal Fallacy**

"If remote work increases, cities die. Cities aren't dying, so remote work isn't increasing."

A. Modus tollens

B. Denying the consequent? (No—actually affirming the negation incorrectly) → Affirming the negation / Invalid converse-style reasoning ✅

C. Sound argument

D. Straw man

**Explanation:** From ¬Q infer ¬P given only P→Q is invalid unless Q→P also holds.

---

**Q10. Evidence Fallacy**

"No study proves aliens don't exist; therefore aliens exist."

A. Red herring

B. Appeal to ignorance ✅

C. Bandwagon

D. Slippery slope

**Explanation:** Absence of disconfirming evidence ≠ evidence of truth.

**Q11. Relevance Fallacy**

B. Ad hominem (abusive/circumstantial) ✅

C. False dichotomy

D. Questionable cause
